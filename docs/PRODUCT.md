# SleepIQ — Product Requirements

**Status:** Approved product scope v1.0 — owner decisions Q1–Q7 incorporated; implementation details deferred to specialised documents  
**Product:** SleepIQ  
**Audience:** Product owner, software architect, coding agents  
**Purpose:** Define *what* SleepIQ must do, independently of implementation choices.

> This document is a requirements source of truth. It is not a coding task list. Agents must not treat open questions or proposed defaults as approved requirements.

## 1. Product vision

SleepIQ is a private, PC-first sleep analytics application that automatically imports Apple Watch measurements through Apple Health, presents a clean, dark-themed overview of the latest night, and supports detailed investigation of long-term trends. It replaces routine manual sleep tracking and provides a transparent, owner-defined SleepIQ Score.

### Primary success criteria

1. **No routine manual data entry:** once configured, incoming sleep measurements can update SleepIQ without spreadsheet entry or manual export.
2. **Trustworthy calculations:** displayed values can be traced to imported source measurements and deterministic calculation definitions.
3. **Useful analytics:** users can chart any supported metric, overlay multiple metrics, save comparison presets, and investigate correlations.
4. **Flexible scoring:** the score formula can be changed after the app is built; the selected version can recompute all historical nights consistently.
5. **Private and resilient:** health data is stored locally by default, can be exported and backed up.

## 2. Intended user and usage

- **User:** One person, using a Windows PC; multi-user support is out of scope.
- **Interface:** Local web application, accessible in the PC browser. Packaging as a desktop installer is not required for v1.
- **Usage frequency:** Mainly periodic review every few days, rather than mandatory daily check-ins.
- **Home environment:** iPhone and Windows PC are typically on the same home network; PC may occasionally be turned off.
- **Refresh expectation:** Eventual delivery is acceptable; immediate morning synchronisation is not required. Actual unattended delivery and recovery should still be verified during integration testing.

## 3. In scope: v1 functional requirements

### 3.1 Automatic data ingestion

- Receive health records generated by **Health Auto Export** over a configurable REST API endpoint.
- Accept the provider's tested **JSON v2** format, including timestamped `sleep_analysis` intervals and physiological readings.
- Include an adapter boundary so another export service can be supported without changing the analytics and scoring layers.
- **Backfill as much available Apple Health history as practical**; report gaps and import limits rather than imposing an arbitrary start date. Support repeated overlapping exports without duplicating measurements.
- Retain source provenance, original timestamps/offsets, and information necessary to reprocess data when calculation rules change.
- Show last successful import, freshness, and useful error states in the app.
- Recover gracefully from PC downtime and delayed or incomplete uploads.
- Provide a way to import past eligible data independently of daily automatic sync. Prior Excel Daily Log is an **optional migration source**, not a requirement to preserve the old proprietary Apple Sleep Score.

### 3.2 Supported measurements

Display available measurements with units, definitions and missing-data indicators:

- Sleep onset (sleep 'bedtime' for this application), final wake time, and total sleep duration.
- Core, Deep, REM, and Awake time; sleep efficiency derived using a documented convention.
- Sleeping heart rate (bpm), derived from readings within the defined overnight sleep interval.
- P34 provides estimated time-weighted sleeping HR with explicit method/coverage/fallback information and owner-authorized, locally validated activation; absent activation retains the existing simple mean. HRV is unchanged. Assumptions/history/gates: [DATA_PIPELINE.md](DATA_PIPELINE.md#proposed-sleeping-hr-time-weighting-p34), [PLAN.md](PLAN.md#sleeping-hr-time-weighting---planning-only-p34-2026-11-01). Weighting is not guaranteed greater accuracy.
- P34 historical activation completed under explicit owner authorization on 2026-11-01 after private numerical/recovery validation; the running app uses the weighted selection and retains old outputs for rollback.
- **Overnight HRV — SDNN (ms)**, derived from valid timestamped HRV readings within the sleep interval; avoid labelling daily HRV as overnight HRV.
- ~~Resting heart rate (bpm)~~ — removed from the dashboard, charts and export by owner decision (P27, U03); still stored.
- Respiratory rate (breaths/min), appropriately scoped to sleep when supported by sample timestamps.
- Apple sleeping wrist temperature, where available, clearly labelled with its source and unit/meaning.
- **Every supported metric in the configured Health Auto Export feed** should be available for inspection and graphing when meaningful; do not silently discard a provided metric. This includes all six categories confirmed in the supplied example and relevant future metrics the owner enables. **Do not expand scope to the full Apple Health catalogue** or unrelated categories without owner approval. Newly encountered field shapes require explicit validation before being marked supported.
- Sleep consistency metrics: timing deviations, 30-day reference schedule, and consistency score.

Measurements that cannot be reliably calculated must be reported as missing or unavailable, never fabricated. Exact overnight aggregation and sleep-session boundary rules will be defined in `DATA_PIPELINE.md`.

### 3.3 Custom SleepIQ scoring

- Overall SleepIQ Score ranges from **0 to 100**, is calculated by deterministic application code, and is visually prominent.
- **Sleep consistency contributes to the overall score from the first version**.
- Consistency compares sleep onset and final wake time with an appropriate baseline calculated from the **preceding 30 calendar days**, excluding the scored night.
- Handle clock times spanning midnight, daylight-saving changes, sparse history, and missing readings explicitly.
- Use **SleepIQ Score v2** (P33): duration 25%, consistency 25%, Deep 20%, REM 20%, sleeping HR 10%, with the approved curves in SCORING.md. Maximum 100; HRV and efficiency remain independent metrics and are not score requirements. Preserve pilot-1 history; formula changes require approval and a new version.
- For v1, editing the isolated **Python scoring module/configuration** is sufficient; dashboard-based formula editing is not required. Explain how to change it safely and how to rerun calculations.
- Formula parameters and definitions must be easy to replace after release; changing the selected formula triggers a consistent historical recalculation.
- Preserve formula version history and make it possible to distinguish scores computed with different versions.
- Preserve original raw data, rather than only the current score.

### 3.4 Dashboard and visual design

- Premium, restrained **dark theme** combining a simple overview with data-dense analytics below.
- Initial viewport focuses on **the latest selected night, including an unscored night with partial/conflicted status**, featuring a large SleepIQ Score and key measurements.
- Scrolling reveals individually accessible graphs and an interactive analytics workspace.
- Interactive date windows: **7, 30 and 90 days, all time and custom dates**.
- Every supported graphable metric has its own individual time-series view.
- Users can overlay **multiple selected metrics on a shared chart** (e.g., sleep score, deep sleep, sleeping heart rate).
- Comparison display supports actual values with clearly labelled units/axes and a normalised-trend view; avoid misleading cross-unit comparisons.
- Optional **7-day and 30-day rolling averages** on supported time-series graphs.
- Interactive tooltips, readable legends, handling of gaps and missing records, and consistent date alignment.
- **Graph presets**: create, name, edit, delete, load and set a default. Presets retain metric selection, date window, display mode and relevant chart options.
- Precise visual tokens, components, states and interactions will be specified in `DESIGN.md`.

### 3.5 Correlation explorer

- Select two supported numeric metrics and view a scatter plot and a statistically computed correlation.
- Include the number of paired observations, units, missing-data rules and a warning about limited samples.
- Never describe correlation as causation.
- Computations run locally, without requiring an LLM call.

### 3.6 AI trend investigator
*Removed in P28 (2026-10-29): the AI assistant no longer exists; see `docs/DECISIONS.md` P28.*

### 3.7 Export, backup and recovery

- Export selected or all supported nightly metrics and current scores to **CSV and/or Excel**.
- Provide automatic local backups of the database to a **separate folder on the same PC**, with a documented restore procedure. An off-device/cloud backup is not required for v1; clearly communicate that same-PC backups do not protect against whole-device loss.
- Avoid including API credentials or secret configuration in backups or exports.
- Preserve raw readings or provide an explicit raw-data export/recovery strategy.

## 4. Explicit exclusions

The following are **not part of SleepIQ**, unless the owner explicitly changes scope:

- No nocturia tracking, including no yes/no control, daily form, notes or historical field.
- No training or workout diary.
- No general daily notes or routine manual check-in.
- No Apple proprietary Sleep Score ingestion, display or historical comparison.
- No AI assistant of any kind (removed in P28), chatbot or AI medical advice.
- No automatic lifestyle recommendations or medical diagnosis.
- No heatmap calendar, arbitrary before/after period comparison feature, unusual-night alerts, or personal-records/achievements feature in v1.
- No mobile app, multi-user accounts, publicly accessible dashboard, autonomous actions, or trading features.

## 5. Non-functional requirements

### Security and privacy
- Local-first storage and least-privilege access.
- Secrets are not committed to Git, logged, displayed in screenshots, or stored in plain-text exports.
- The temporary LAN HTTP receiver with its short test token **must not be reused as production security**. Secure authenticated transport and stronger credentials are required for long-running operation.
- Incoming payload size, type and contents are validated; failures do not corrupt existing records.
- No LLM tool can modify the health database or execute arbitrary code.
- Security controls and permitted exceptions are defined in `SECURITY.md`.

### Accuracy and reliability
- Imports are repeatable and idempotent.
- Every nightly metric has a reproducible calculation rule and test cases.
- Session grouping and duration calculations use timezone-aware instants, including the UK autumn daylight-saving transition on 25 October 2026.
- Calculation changes must not silently rewrite raw observations.
- Pending/partial/unavailable nights are distinguishable from complete nights.

### Testability and maintainability
- Backend unit/integration tests: **Pytest**.
- Frontend tests: component/unit tests as appropriate.
- Real browser end-to-end tests: **Playwright** for dashboard, chart interactions, presets, and data freshness/error states.
- Security checks, static checks, data integrity fixtures, and CI quality gates are mandatory.
- Project documentation should support **progressive disclosure**: concise `AGENTS.md` plus task-specific Markdown files; no mandatory `CLAUDE.md`.
- Coding agents must be able to work in controlled phases, with model recommendations by complexity, risk and uncertainty.
- **Owner learning and control are product-development requirements**, not optional commentary. Agents must teach relevant concepts in plain English and invite the owner to make meaningful decisions. See Section 6.

## 6. Owner involvement, learning and decision protocol

The owner is **actively learning software engineering while building SleepIQ**. Agents are collaborators and teachers, **not autonomous product owners**. The quality of the experience includes the owner's understanding of key design decisions.

### Mandatory decision checkpoint

Before a consequential or difficult-to-reverse choice (architecture, dependencies, data model, scoring semantics, privacy, authentication, external services, cost, deployment, UI direction, or significant scope change), the agent must:

1. **Identify the decision** in plain English, and explain *why it matters* in 1–3 sentences, defining unfamiliar terms.
2. Present **2–3 viable options** where there is a meaningful choice; describe practical **pros, cons, security/privacy implications, maintenance burden and cost** where relevant. Make a recommendation and distinguish facts from assumptions.
3. Ask the owner to **choose or approve** the option. Do not silently select a consequential approach, and **do not implement it until approved**.
4. Once approved, capture the outcome and rationale in `DECISIONS.md` and, when necessary, update the relevant design or architecture document.
5. Show what changed, tests executed, how to try it, and a short **what you learned** explanation at the end of each milestone.

### Avoid unnecessary interruptions

- Agents may independently handle **routine, reversible implementation details** that follow approved architecture and standards. Examples: names of private helper functions, simple refactors, or formatting.
- **Batch related decisions** into short numbered questions at the start of a phase; do not ask for permission on every line of code. Stop only at genuine approval gates.
- When uncertain whether a choice affects behavior, money, privacy, security, or long-term maintainability, **surface it** rather than bury it in implementation.
- Prefer concise, progressive explanations. Offer deeper details on request; never drown the owner in unexplained jargon or enormous one-shot prompts.
- Never interpret lack of reply as approval. Never weaken tests or security to eliminate an approval gate.

### Progressive disclosure for coding agents

- Read `AGENTS.md` first; then load **only the task-relevant documentation** rather than every file on every turn.
- State task scope, assumptions, intended files, acceptance tests and approval gates before substantial changes.
- Use small, verifiable increments with explicit handoffs. Provide relevant diffs and test results; avoid repetitively re-reading context or producing oversized prompts.
- Model assignments (Luna/Sol/Astra and effort levels) are **proposals** based on complexity, risk and ambiguity, subject to the owner's approval when they change workflow or budget.

## 7. Acceptance scenarios for the product

1. A valid API upload arrives; the database updates, and the latest selected night displays accurately, including missing-input and partial/conflicted states.
2. The same seven-day export arrives twice; each source observation and nightly result appears only once.
3. Sleep phases spanning midnight and a DST transition produce correct elapsed durations and dated nightly sessions.
4. A night with no HRV data displays HRV as missing and applies the scoring formula's documented missing-data policy.
5. Changing the active formula version recomputes all eligible historical nights without destroying source records or previous formula definitions.
6. A user overlays sleep score, deep sleep and sleeping HR, changes date range, turns on a rolling average and saves the configuration as a preset.
7. The correlation explorer reports a reproducible correlation and paired-night count, including sensible handling of sparse data.
8. The charts and statistics need no external service.
9. A backup can be restored into a test environment; an exported dataset can be opened without proprietary software.
10. A Windows PC restart or delayed upload does not cause duplicated or corrupted records.

## 8. Resolved owner decisions

### Approved on 28 October 2026

- **Q1:** The score can be edited in a Python module/configuration for now; UI-based formula editing is deferred.
- **Q2:** Use a simple, clearly labelled development formula and refine it later with owner approval.
- **Q3:** Import all practical available historical Apple Health records; use the earlier Excel Daily Log as an optional supplement/fallback for underlying metrics.
- **Q4:** Include sleeping wrist temperature and all other metrics in the configured export, with honest data definitions and availability handling.
- **Q5:** Same-PC local backups are sufficient for the initial release.
- **Q6:** Astra may compare and recommend a secure approach for iPhone-to-PC transport, including HTTPS or a practical encrypted alternative. **Astra must present the options and obtain owner approval before implementation**; approval to evaluate is not approval to deploy a specific option.
- **Process:** The owner wants to be involved in decisions and learn why choices are made. Agents must present options, pros/cons and recommendations at meaningful checkpoints rather than working autonomously through architectural decisions.

### Approved on 28 October 2026 — Q7 scope clarification

- **Q7 (approved):** “All available metrics” means **all metrics enabled in the configured Health Auto Export feed** (the six confirmed categories, plus relevant future metrics the owner adds). **It does not mean** attempting to ingest every Apple Health category regardless of relevance. Newly added metrics must be mapped and validated rather than silently assumed compatible.

### Implementation questions, not product-scope blockers

Detailed design choices (authentication, database structure, data retention, backup scheduling, app launch and packaging, and session aggregation semantics) are **not implicitly approved** by this scope sign-off. Present options with pros/cons and obtain owner approval in the relevant phase as prescribed in Section 6.
